# Junction lag plan review

Reviewed against the worktree at `a076419ca`, the saved coil trace, and solver and checkpoint code on 2026-11-18. Revise the design before implementation. The trace supports investigating the junction delay. Two existing contracts prevent approval of the proposed interval-by-interval resize.

1. **P1 — Repeated resizing breaks the D46 lifetime accounting.** In one accepted step, the component term is the change in buffer content, proportional to `m × Δw`. With constant mass these terms telescope over the buffer's life. Wash a junction from pure A to pure B at 10 kg, resize to 1 kg, wash back to A, and restore its original mass. Its initial and final buffer are identical, yet the two washes transfer a net 9 kg of A to the physical streams and take 9 kg of B. Repeating the cycle grows the discrepancy. Enthalpy has the same problem. Physical total mass can still balance, and every individual step can pass. Preserve the existing D46 lifetime bound or present a replacement conservation contract for owner approval. Add a multi-interval alternating-flow and composition test, including solids and an enthalpy change. Retain the existing water-wash gate. 2. **P1 — Certified replay bypasses the resize.** A retained worker can restart a solve from saved endpoint flows. An active certificate instead extrapolates a recorded inventory delta, including the junction's, from the certificate base. A resize inside the measured interval could therefore be repeated as a transport delta. Old certificates could also keep operating under the old mixing law. Specify certificate eligibility across a resize, how to handle existing certificates through the declared replay revision, and how wake-up restores the accepted state. Compare an always-solving island with certificate replay, wake-up and a save/reopen with a live certificate. Exact checkpoints already contain buffer amounts, energy, solids and endpoint flows when a last result exists. Use the cold fallback only when usable history is absent. 3. **P2 — The cap and lag bounds need explicit conditions.** The current cap takes the largest eligible connection using seed density. Two equal incoming and two equal outgoing branches at their individual caps give total through-flow twice the largest connection. The proposed clamp then makes `dt × Q / m` twice its claimed ceiling at steady flow. Even on a single line, a tenfold flow drop gives a tenfold longer first-interval lag when mass uses the previous endpoint flow and both flows are above the floor. A node with no eligible pipe has zero cap, for which the existing mint supplies a positive 1 kg fallback. Define the fallback, which flow terms enter the sum, and when density is sampled. Test a balanced manifold, a flow step in each direction, and a heat-only or isolated junction. 4. **P2 — The measurement and acceptance test use different temperatures.** The trace records the 638.15 K setpoint, firing share, interval-average flow and endpoint node states. At tick 1000 one coil reports 80.452 kg/s and a 534.62 K receiving junction. Delivered stream enthalpy and its downstream-pressure flash temperature are absent. Firing at 1 selects the donor heat curve's setpoint enthalpy. The column's displayed feed uses cycle-integrated enthalpy and the table slope. Test the transported enthalpy per unit mass, the junction endpoint at its actual pressure, and the column feed over the same committed period. Specify the starting state and accepted step sequence for the ±0.5 K criterion. For constant heat capacity, a single 20 s backward-Euler step at a 0.2 s time constant retains 1/101 of the old gap, or 1.03 K from the observed 103.53 K gap. Shorter substeps can pass a tighter threshold.
