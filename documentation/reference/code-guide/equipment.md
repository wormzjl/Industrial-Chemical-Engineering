## Native service housings, probes and finish (0.22.0)

`EquipmentServices` owns the physical enclosure, mounting brackets, DATA/FE datums and solids. `ServiceSurface` queries native facets; it does not substitute an equipment bounding box for a support. The plan is imported by `ShapeRasterizer` before placement/collision tables are made. Rendering, constructor ghosts, world registration and the drawing exporter read that same plan. Coarse meshes retain Normal case/socket datums and reproject bracket contacts onto their own facets.

Horizontal drums use a saddle side; spheres use a clamped service leg; floating tanks use the front gauge/service area. Heater mounts avoid native ladder clearance. Pump/compressor cases fit the drive-end deck. Rotating DATA sides follow shaft power flow, with FE opposite; large motor/generator cases are matched. The turbine reuses its existing governor enclosure. Inline movers have integrated terminal necks and the control valve a side-facing positioner. `SERVICE_CASE` and `SERVICE_COVER` use equipment BODY paint; connector identity rings and instrument steel keep their own finishes.

Gate fixes (batch `2026-11-09-main-gate-fixes`): a pad case gives way to every nozzle's neck and flange at its largest pipe (`EquipmentServices.necks`; a column has none, its bores follow its place). `fitPad` slides along the carrier, then tries the inward leg faces, then slides up or down the carrier down to a 0.2 × 0.16 × 0.1 m junction box; where no pad clears the necks, the case stays where the frame holds it. A bracket roots on its carrier behind the case's back: a face the ray meets in front of it is no root, and the bracket falls back up or down the carrier, else to the anchor's plane. A low-voltage motor's or generator's 0.4 × 0.3 × 0.3 m case (`CASE_ACROSS`, `CASE_HEIGHT`, `CASE_ALONG`, `caseFloor`) stands over its terminal box's point. `ProcessUnit.serviceCase` adds its top corners to the frame points, so the frame box holds the case, as a large machine's cabinet does.

`EquipmentInstruments` is the single probe-location/manual-policy catalog. `InstrumentGeometry` adapts it for mounts and supplies shared transmitter poses/preview heads. `InstrumentWiring` supplies reinforced roots, short 20 mm protected metallic drops and long surface-textured runs. `SurfaceSection`/`SurfaceJoints` follow actual facets and connected supports. Branches share an orthogonal baseline; curved-facet strips retain full width and a continuous texture station. These finishes add no process nodes or physical collision. Long-run geometry is built lazily for rendering, not by server process ticks.

Constructor previews include factory caps and level heads. Manual instruments remain accessory facts and appear when fitted; drawings explicitly show those mount options fitted for review. The native book/exporter is `tools/equipment-drawings/`; historical Python proposals are labelled separately and are not overlaid on implemented geometry. The 96-entry layout corpus records 14 cell/stamp changes from this native-interface addition, with equipment boxes, process-nozzle cells and shaft terminals retained.


## Construction and layout additions (0.22.0)

The exchanger remains one equipment kind with catalogued Standard/Low-dP crossflow sub-types. Shapes and constructor designs append the selected type; missing type is Standard. `compat/equipment/shapes-9.snbt`, `shapes-10.snbt`, constructor design images and `compat/layouts/` pin saved facts and nozzle layouts. Protocol is `equipment-21`. Rebuilds preserve identity, contents, paint, origin and wired/nozzle endpoints; never overwrite another block.

Tank gauge is now integrated as an optional accessory, with a narrower chamber (`TankGaugeGeometry.CHAMBER_RADIUS` 0.10 m). `TankDesign` defines compact as D < 6 m and shell H < 6 m. Rim walkway requires D ≥ 6 m; rolling roof access additionally requires non-compact and H ≤ D. Without rolling access, a fixed transparent safety grid covers the opening and moving-roof player collision is omitted. The ring and gauger landing share rim height, face the straight external ladder and carry inner handrails; there is no intermediate external platform.

Manway openings use 500 mm below shell D 1.25 m, 600 mm generally and 750 mm at D ≥ 4 m; D 0.9–1 m can fall back to 450 mm, and smaller shells omit one. These are game layout bands. Common nozzle/flange finish and bolt bands match pipe connections. The centrifugal compressor is a compact barrel with larger top inlet, smaller top outlet, guarded shaft and no coolant connection; the owner approved rebuilding its moved connections.

Short reducer stencils use their available exposed surface and are omitted on a side whose corners meet the casing. The geometric probes follow the actual reducer radius and shortened hub; retained placement frames may have empty edge cells after approved platform/coolant removal.

`LineGeometry` draws off-centre tees/crosses with fitted branch sweeps to their saved face coordinates; centred junctions retain their existing path. `SocketGeometry` reads the shared service housing sockets and draws their glands; wired remounts preserve the old entry UUID/cell/face/offsets. Cabinet and sensor finishes are generated by `ControlAssets` in the workstation palette; this changes no equipment envelope.

Constructor initial dimensions are interior defaults and its paint scheme is user-specific. Its collision warning stays visible away from the aimed cell. Constructor/simulator models are authored in `tools/pump-sprites/`, with matching collision boxes and four facing variants. `ModelledBlockEntity` centralises geometry/model-data refresh for equipment, pipes, racks and conduits. Data/FE service mounts are on casings or cabinets with clearance; see [wired-control-power.md](wired-control-power.md).

## Optional client render detail (0.22.0)

`RenderDetail.NORMAL|LOW` selects drawing only. Client config `equipment.low_polygon_mode` in `icheme-client.toml` defaults false. Mixed-detail clients share the same server facts, shape, collision, layout stamps, nozzle/shaft positions and calculations. Normal pump/compressor cover faces use `PUMP_COVER` plus twelve raised nuts recessed within the prior envelope; Low keeps the earlier `PUMP_DISC` texture and flat textured flange nuts (`BOLT_FACE`).

`ShapeRasterizer` keeps canonical geometry separate from its bounded coarse cache; coarse meshes reuse physical registration/reach tables. Round primitives cap angular samples at 24, heads at six bands and spheres at 12 latitude bands; small pipe/shaft profiles remain unchanged. `EquipmentBodyRenderer` and `LoadedEquipment` generalise the tower's whole-mesh renderer/index so coarse facets spanning interior cells cannot disappear at chunk-cell boundaries. `EquipmentBodyRenderer` merges the bodies whose origins share a 32-block square into one static mesh and draws each square in view with one call (batch 2026-11-09-lod-rendering: one draw per structure cost Low detail 0.8 ms over 250 structures; merged, Low equals Normal's frame time on a discrete GPU). Bodies are cached by shape, paint and detail (32); a frame compares each square's members by identity and merges at most one changed square, its last mesh standing meanwhile; each structure's light is read once a second. Interfaces/markings/glass remain in chunk models. Moving parts, roof/ladder frames, preview and visible liquids follow the active profile. ## Protected removal and constructor deconstruction (0.22.0)

`EquipmentProtection` grants exact-cell, level-specific, server-thread authority only inside verified structure lifecycle edits and Creative-player removal. `EquipmentChunkMixin`, declared in `icheme.mixins.json` and the mod metadata, refuses other `LevelChunk.setBlockState` writes before state or block entities change. Shell/interior blocks have negative hardness, high blast resistance, immovable piston reaction, and reject entity/custom explosion destruction. Layout refresh, retirement and interior restatement use the same scoped authority; no permission survives an exception.

`EquipmentDeconstruction.target` finds the nearest loaded structure crossing the constructor’s right/up placement line, at most 16 blocks ahead, independently of the selected draft kind or dimensions. The GUI names the target and cell count. `Deconstruct` carries its origin, transient shape stamp and request ID; the server reselects it, checks reach/build rights, loaded cells and every cell’s break-permission event before any removal. `DeconstructAnswer` preserves the request ID. A changed or denied target leaves the whole structure untouched. No saved field or layout identity changes.

Removal reuses `EquipmentStructure.dismantle`: assembly/shaft invalidation and queued fluid removal remain on their existing paths. Connected pipes/cables and unrelated blocks remain. Survival recovers installed accessories; process contents leave with the equipment through the existing removal ledger. Deconstruction performs no process solve or presentation flush.

- You change how a drum, a distillation column, a floating-roof tank, a storage sphere or a process unit is shaped, drawn, collided with, climbed or placed.
- You add a unit kind, a nozzle, a deck, a stair, a ladder or a moving part.
- You change the drawn shape of a pipe, a pipe fitting (valves, sight glass, Y-strainer, inline pump and compressor), a gauge dial or a constructed mover's flow arrows.
- You touch the equipment constructor, its packets (`equipment-21`), its Import of a process simulator's designs (`SentDesign`, `SentDesignTags`) or the shell cells' NBT.
- You change a column's component design (`ColumnComponents`, `ColumnDesign`, the member counts `ColumnComponents.Units`), where a column's side nozzle stands or which side it faces, or a nozzle's flow direction and arrows (`PortMarkings`). The plant built round a column is in [machine-assemblies.md](machine-assemblies.md).
- A device's name is lost, refused or drawn wrong: the name rule (`DeviceName`), its NBT (`DeviceNames`), the rename path (`EquipmentNetwork.Rename`, `EquipmentStructure.rename`) and the nameplate (`Nameplate`, `NameplateRenderer`). The name entry on the screens is in [client-gui.md](client-gui.md) (`TitleStrip`).
- A pump, compressor, steam turbine, motor, generator or gearbox has the wrong size for its design point (`MachineDuty`), its shaft end does not meet a shaft block, or its terminal cell (FE port) is in the wrong place. Trains and power are in [rotating-machinery.md](rotating-machinery.md).
- A placed vessel looks wrong after a code change (the layout refresh), or a pipe does not meet a nozzle.
- A tool refuses a pipe at a column's nozzle as too large: the largest pipe a nozzle takes (`NozzleBore`).
- A log shows `equipment_compat` (a saved shape repaired, a retired kind removed) or `equipment_refresh` lines; you rename, remove or add a `Kind`, `Form`, `Heading` or `Orientation` constant or a shape field.
- A reservoir's or a constructed mover's fluid registration (ports, volume, level height, open flag) is wrong. The fluid side is in [fluid-runtime.md](fluid-runtime.md).

## In one paragraph

A player places an **equipment constructor** and designs a vessel on its screen: a drum (standing or lying), a column laid out by its trays, a floating-roof tank, a storage sphere, or one of 26 unit kinds (exchanger, air cooler, pump, compressor, heaters, reactors, steam turbine, motor, generator, gearbox, cooling towers, FCC converter, delayed coker, boilers, gasifiers). **Build** sends the design. **Import** lists the designs the nearest process simulator holds (the block that replaced the column calculator): the column design its Save design stored, then each unit design its Send to block stored (`SentDesign`: a tag, a member key, a count in parallel, the shape, a column's design); the chosen one lands on the draft with its name. A column is built only from an imported column design (or a column template): its diameter, trays, components and member counts; the player keeps the skirt, the placement, the paint and which side a nozzle faces. A structure or one-block device may carry a **name**, a fact on its block entities like its paint: an import's name goes to the built structure, a rename changes it, and a named structure draws a nameplate. The server places the vessel whole, as plain blocks: **shell** cells, which carry the shape and draw their own piece of the surface, and invisible **interior** cells. Every surface polygon is clipped to its own cell. A chunk therefore bakes each shell cell like a normal block, from a **cell key**, and no cell reads another cell or chunk. Collision is each cell's solid at 1/8 block. Nozzles take the size of the pipe connected to them, up to a pipe's largest; a column's nozzle takes at most the largest pipe its place clears. A complete drum, tank or sphere registers as one fluid reservoir with its design volume, its level height and a port per nozzle; the tank registers open to the atmosphere. A complete pump, compressor or centrifugal compressor registers one mover at its suction nozzle cell with one suction and one discharge port. A complete exchanger, kettle, air cooler or fired heater registers one **heat side** per passage, each at its first inlet's cell with its inlet and outlet nozzles as ports: an exchanger's tubes and shell, a kettle's tubes, an air cooler's process side, a fired heater's convection and radiant coils; a kettle's shell registers after them as a closed vessel at its pool's node ([heat-exchangers.md](heat-exchangers.md)). Columns and the other units are drawn only; a column and the units round it form an assembly that the server validates against the column's design ([machine-assemblies.md](machine-assemblies.md)). Using a shell cell is decided on the server: a column, or a unit a column's report holds, opens the column's assembly screen; anything else opens its own screen. A tank's floating roof and rolling ladder are drawn by the client and collide through `FloatingRoofs`, at the height the tank's published level gives. Only a Creative player can break a cell, which dismantles the whole structure. Survival uses the constructor’s Deconstruct action; its existing instrument-removal path recovers accessories. A saved shape is a fact (AGENTS.md, "Saves and data formats"): it is read by name onto the current rules and repaired where they refuse it (`ShapeRepair`); a structure an older layout placed is rebuilt to the current one when it loads, its nozzles kept on their pipes first, and a structure of a kind this build no longer has is removed whole. The geometry code (`science/equipment`) is Minecraft-free and deterministic; it also draws pipes, pipe fittings and gauge dials. The world side is `EquipmentStructure`, `FloatingRoofs` and two block entities. Process contents and operating state belong to the fluid runtime: constructor packets edit static layouts, and process edits and displays follow the engine schedule.

## Main flow

```mermaid
sequenceDiagram
    participant S as EquipmentConstructorScreen
    participant N as EquipmentNetwork (server)
    participant C as EquipmentConstructorBlockEntity
    participant E as EquipmentStructure
    participant F as FluidWorldAuthority
    S->>N: ConstructorPayload(design, build=true)
    N->>C: Design(...) validates, apply(design)
    N->>E: clashes() empty? place(level, origin, shape, paint, all rows)
    E->>E: shell cells bind(shape, cell, paint); interior cells
    E->>F: reservoir: placeVessel(node, ports, volume, levelHeight, open); mover: placeMover(suction, kind, ports) [ledger edit]
    E->>E: reconnect pipes, refreshNozzles
    N-->>S: chat message (built / blocked / clash)
```

1. The screen edits a local draft and sends it as a `ConstructorPayload` with what to do with it: closing sends it with `KEEP`, **Build** with `BUILD`. **Import** is two requests, each after a `KEEP` of the draft (batch 2026-11-02-process-simulator, S5). `ImportList` reads the nearest process simulator within `IMPORT_REACH` 16 (`simulator`, `ProcessSimulatorBlockEntity.listed`: its saved column design first, untagged, then what Send to block stored); every list is answered with an `ImportListAnswer` (`no_calculator`, `no_design` or `not_editable` when empty). The player picks one; `ImportChoice` lands it (`Design.landed`) on the draft just applied, with the name the page listed (none for the untagged column design), if the listed index still holds that tag and member (else `import_changed`). Every choice is answered with an `ImportAnswer`: the message (`imported` for the saved column design, naming the trays and feed in the calculator's numbering: trays + 2, feed + 1; `imported_column` or `imported_unit` with the name, the kind and the count to build) and the design after it (the `writeDesign` tags), or only why there is none (`no_calculator`, `import_changed`, `invalid`, `not_editable`). The screen loads the design whole or shows the refusal on its status line until the next edit (to chat when the screen has closed).
2. `EquipmentNetwork` checks the player (loaded, within 8 blocks, may build, not a spectator, may interact). It rebuilds the `Design` (`ConstructorPayload.design`), whose compact constructor validates the shape, and keeps the constructor's imported column (`keepingColumn`: a packet never sets it). An invalid design is answered in chat (an import's in its answer).
3. A column without an imported design is refused (`import_first`), and so is one whose side nozzle finds no row on the constructor's own skirt (`ColumnDesign.misfit`, chat `misfit`). A build with side-connection clashes is refused. Otherwise `EquipmentStructure.place` checks every cell first: loaded, inside the build height, replaceable. The first blocked cell is named in an `IllegalStateException`, and nothing is placed.
4. Cells are set with `UPDATE_CLIENTS | UPDATE_KNOWN_SHAPE` (no neighbour shape updates). Each shell cell binds its shape, cell, paint, the design's name, the layout's stamp and box.
5. A whole vessel with `fluidDevice()` registers through one queued ledger edit. A drum, tank or sphere: `FluidWorldAuthority.placeVessel` at its lowest inside cell, a port per nozzle with the class its `PortClass` gives (a tank's OVERFLOW nozzle becomes an OVERFLOW port), open for a tank. A vessel over `FluidDeviceSpec.MAX_VOLUME` (30 000 m³) is logged and not registered. A pump, compressor or centrifugal compressor: `FluidWorldAuthority.placeMover` at its suction nozzle cell with its SUCTION and DISCHARGE nozzles as ports (auxiliary nozzles, such as an oil cooler's, stay drawn only). A heat unit: `FluidWorldAuthority.placeUnit` with one `UnitSide` per `HeatSides.of` side (HEAT_SIDE at its first inlet's cell, its inlet and outlet nozzles as INLET and OUTLET ports, its passage, a `HeatUnit` with its side and surface) and, for a kettle, a `UnitVessel` at its pool's node with the shell's own nozzles. The node cell takes the identity (`bindIdentity`); a heat unit's every node takes its side's. Then pipes in front of the ports update their connections, and the nozzle cells take the pipes' sizes.
6. Use on any cell is decided on the server (`EquipmentShellBlock.use`). A column, or a unit a column's assembly report holds, opens that column's assembly screen ([machine-assemblies.md](machine-assemblies.md)). Otherwise a reservoir or constructed mover opens its fluid menu (`FluidDeviceMenu`, reach widened by the vessel's size) with a datasheet tab, and another unit gets the datasheet: the server sends `assembly_datasheet` and the client opens `VesselScreen` from the cells it already has. The fluid debugger always opens the device's own screen. Breaking any cell calls `dismantle`.
7. **Names** (N1). A screen's name entry sends `Rename(position, name)` ([client-gui.md](client-gui.md), `TitleStrip`). The server checks the player as for an edit (loaded, may build, not a spectator, may interact) and the reach of the device's screen (`FluidDeviceMenu.REACH`, plus the structure's largest size). A one-block device takes it when `nameable()`; a structure takes it on every shell cell (`EquipmentStructure.rename`), or not at all while a cell is not loaded (`rename_unloaded` on the action bar). The text is cleaned (`DeviceName.clean`); empty clears the name. A name is a drawing fact, not process state: no engine event, the block entities send it to the clients as they send paint. A refresh keeps it. The client draws the plate of a named structure (`NameplateRenderer`, `Nameplate.of`) and the tag of a named one-block device; an unnamed one draws nothing.

<details><summary>Frames, boxes and units</summary>

- 1 block = 1 m. A vessel is built in its own frame, axis along +y. Vertical: the front (liquid outlet, skirt opening) is frame −z. Lying drum: frame x is up, y the axis, the front head at y = 0. A unit's frame stands like a vertical vessel's, its front −z towards the constructor.
- `width()` is odd, so the axis runs through cell centres. Every nozzle's centre line does too, and its flange face lies on a block face (`STAND_OUT` 0.15 m at least beyond the surface).
- `transform()` turns the frame by quarter turns to face `front` and shifts the box to start at 0. The origin is the box's lower north-west corner.
- The **controller** is a role, not a block: the lowest shell cell, then westmost, then northmost.
- Vessel grid: drum D 0.5..10 m, column D 0.5..15 m, both in 0.5 m steps; tangent length 1..64 whole m (a column's follows its trays); support up to `MAX_SUPPORT` 16 (a skirt at least `minimumSkirt(D)`, room under the bottom head for the outlet elbow at the largest pipe: 2 m to D 2.5, 3 m to D 6.5, 4 m to D 10.5; or 0 for a bare standing drum; saddles at least 1, centred `SADDLE_POSITION` 1/8 of T/T from each tangent line). A tank, a sphere and the units read diameter, tangent length and support as their own sizes (`UnitDesign`): a tank D 4..40 and shell 4..24 whole m, no support; a sphere D 4..24 whole m on a support of 2..8 m.
- **Drum outlets.** A skirted drum's bottom outlet leaves through its skirt port at the front or at the side the player turns it to (`outlet`, quarter turns; `EquipmentShape.outlets` lists the allowed sides: every side for a skirted drum, the front for a column, a design's own for a tank or a unit); the port, slots and chairs turn with it. A lying drum's outlets all leave at its front end and its feed enters at the far end. A lying drum may take a water boot (the constructor's boot switch) where `bootFor` finds room: every drum from 6 m long and at least as long as wide. The boot is a third of D, just inboard of the front saddle; the oil leaves through a bottom nozzle between the boot and the front head, or low on the front head where there is no room. Where no cell there keeps a pipe's largest flange 0.15 m clear of the front saddle, the nozzle stands on the cell beside the saddle's block column and takes a smaller largest bore (`Boot.largest`, 0.05 m steps; 0.5 m on the eight drum sizes this applies to, 2 x 6 m among them; owner decision 2026-10-28). Without a boot the liquid outlet is low on the front head.
- Shell facets: `max(12, 4⌈k π D⌉)`, k = 0.75 for a drum, 1 for a column. Heads have facets/4 bands.
</details>

<details><summary>Contract: cell keys and nozzle codes</summary>

- `CellKey(shape, x, y, z, nozzles)` is the model's and the collision's key. A **body** cell (wholly in the cylindrical shell or skirt, clear of supports, openings, slots, platforms, manway, boot and every nozzle's reach) replaces its axial coordinate with `BODY − class` (`BODY` = −1, class 0..7: skirt or shell, times four course phases). Callers caching by key build each class once. Cells in a ladder's box column never share a body key.
- `nozzles` holds a 7-bit code for each nozzle reaching the cell, in nozzle order: `PipeSizes.code(od)` (1..64, 1/64 m), 0 = unconnected (drawn at its default size under a blind flange). At most 9 nozzles (`KEYED_NOZZLES`, 63 bits) may reach one cell; a 10th throws when the geometry is built.
- A nozzle's cells are those of its largest size (`PipeSizes.MAX_OUTSIDE` 0.8 m), so the layout never depends on what is connected. Every nozzle neck but a column's, a unit's included, can take a pipe's largest size.
- **A column's nozzle takes the largest pipe its place clears** (`NozzleBore`, owner decision 2026-10-27, option C of `NOZZLE_ROOM_GATE_REVIEW`). F3 sizes a nozzle's room by its design bore, so at a pipe's largest a neck can meet a ladder, a rail or the cladding. The search runs from the design pipe up in 0.05 m steps and stops at the first neck that meets the rest of the column; a neck whose drawn outside reaches `MAX_OUTSIDE` gives `MAX_BORE`. The laying tool's anchor refuses a larger line (`PipeAnchor.Refusal.NOZZLE_SIZE`) and its port label shows the nozzle's own largest ([pipe-laying.md](pipe-laying.md)); the sizing tool refuses a pipe or a line beside a nozzle that takes less (`nozzle_too_small`, `PhysicalRegistry.NOZZLE_TOO_SMALL`; [instrument-accessories.md](instrument-accessories.md)). Pinned on the gated columns: the 0.5 m column's side nozzles 0.25 m (the neck wider than the cladding), the 1.5 m column's reflux 0.7 m, the 3 m column's reflux 0.55 m (a ladder beside it); the others a pipe's largest.
- The server writes the codes (`refreshNozzles`: the connected pipe's `PipeSizes.outside(bore)`, 0 without a pipe) and syncs a cell whose key changed. `refreshNozzlesAt` runs from `EquipmentShellBlock.neighborChanged` (next server task) and from `FluidDeviceBlockEntity.refreshGeometry`.
- A pipe connects to a shell cell only where `nozzleFacing(face)` is non-null and the pipe has a joint on that face, as the network joins them (the drawing reads the registry, `FluidDeviceBlock.connectedState`), for every kind. A hand-placed pipe has every face; a laid one only the faces its route uses ([pipe-laying.md](pipe-laying.md)). Only a reservoir's ports and a constructed mover's suction and discharge exist in the fluid topology.
- Every kind's IN and OUT nozzle necks carry one band of four flow arrows each (`PortMarkings.surface`, pointing with the stream that `PortMarkings.flow` gives the role), baked with the cell; the band stops where the neck is hidden (`ShapeRasterizer.hides`), and a code of 0 draws it at the design size. A storage tank's or sphere's ports carry none. They are drawing only; the assemblies read the same `flow` ([machine-assemblies.md](machine-assemblies.md)).
</details>

<details><summary>Contract: collision at 1/8 block, ladders, light</summary>

- `ShapeRasterizer.solid(key)` samples the cell at the centres of 8³ voxels (`SOLID_RESOLUTION` 8) and greedy-merges them (z, then x, then y). `EquipmentStructure.collision` joins the boxes into one `VoxelShape`; both sides compute the same shape. An unbound shell cell is a full block.
- A wall a player must meet is at least one voxel thick: `Access.RAIL_BAND` 0.2, `RUNG_FACE` 0.06, `NARROW_BAND` 0.13 between two fences, `FENCE` 1.4 m high. `Access.PLAYER` and `MIN_DECK` (1.45 m) follow from the voxel size.
- `isLadder` asks `ShapeRasterizer.climbable` at the entity's position (in front of the rungs, inside the cage). Every cell a climb zone reaches is a shell cell, drawn or not (`climbCells`).
- A ladder lands through an opening in its deck's handrail, `Access.WALK_THROUGH` (0.425 m) either side of its line: a player and one voxel each side. A wound stair (`Access.WoundStair`, on tanks and spheres) has a fence either side and is open on its inner side where it meets a deck.
- Shell cells are transparent to light (`getLightBlock` 0, skylight passes). Interior cells are opaque and cast the shadow, except a tank's (`EquipmentInteriorBlock.OPEN`): they pass light so the roof shows from above, collide only up to the roof's walking surface (`FloatingRoofs`), and are a ladder along the rolling ladder's line.
- **A skirt that shows its inside** (`ShapeRasterizer.skylight`: a drum's or a column's hollow skirt, a unit's skirt with openings, `ProcessUnit.openSkirt`; owner request 2026-10-27, batch 2026-10-27-skirt-shadow): the vessel's interior cells are `EquipmentInteriorBlock.SKYLIGHT`. They stay solid and hide the faces beside them, but pass light, so the floor inside the skirt takes the skylight of the open ground beside the vessel. Opaque, they left that floor lit only from the skirt ring, one level less a block inwards (level 9, 54 % at noon, at a 10 m drum's centre). A save from before reads `skylight=false`; the controller cell's load queues `EquipmentStructure.restateLater`, and `restate` (one vessel a tick, after retired and stale ones, once its chunks are loaded and block-ticking; only a current, own vessel) sets each interior cell holding another state, then waits for light (`waitForLightBeforeSending`). A refresh restates the interior cells it keeps. No cell is added or removed and the stamp does not change. Log line `equipment_restate kind=… origin=… interior=…`.
- The roof and the rolling ladder stand where the tank's published level puts them (`TankDisplay.Glide`, the same height on server and client at the same game tick); before the first display the roof stands at the node cell's saved `RoofLevel`. The first display after a load glides from that level over one bucket (`FloatingRoofs.standing`, `TankDisplay.Glide.next`), so a plant that filled or drained while its chunk was unloaded carries its riders up or lets them follow down, as `LIFT` (0.6 m) allows; it does not jump. A rising roof lifts what stands on it (`FloatingRoofs.carry`, from `LevelTickEvent.Post` on the server and the client tick for the player).
</details>

<details><summary>Contract: saved shapes and retired kinds (ShapeRepair)</summary>

AGENTS.md, "Saves and data formats": an equipment shape, its paint and nozzle sizes are facts. Shape fields are append-only, and enums are saved by name.

- **Read by name.** `EquipmentShellBlockEntity.saved(tag)` takes the fields as written (`ShapeRepair.Saved`: names, numbers, null for an absent array); `ShapeRepair.read` gives a `Read`: a valid shape or a retired kind name, and each repair as `field from -> to`. It never throws. A `Kind`, `Heading`, `Orientation` or `Form` name goes through its alias map (one entry at this snapshot: `CONDENSING_TURBINE` reads `STEAM_TURBINE` as a kind and as a form, owner decision D5; a machine's `Drive` and `Steam` have empty maps). A missing field reads its default, the behaviour before the field existed: a drum saved without `Connections` gets `EquipmentShape.defaultConnections` (the standard inlet), with no repair named.
- **Repairs.** An unknown front reads north, an unknown drum orientation vertical, undecodable trays 20 fed at 10, undecodable connections none, an undecodable bank one bay. Then, while the shape is refused, the ladder runs in order: sizes (a unit snapped to its own grid and range, a drum or column to 0.5 m and its range, the length a whole number in range; non-finite values take the kind's default), support into its range (a boot drum's at least `minimumBootSupport`), connections off the shell or past `MAX_CONNECTIONS` dropped, the outlet to the front, the boot removed, the bank to one bay, and last the kind's default size with the saved front. A repaired shape has another stamp, so the normal refresh rebuilds the structure.
- **Retired kind** (owner decision D6). A kind name this build does not have: the cell stays unbound, keeps its saved tag (`retiredData`, written back unchanged), and its `onLoad` (server) queues `EquipmentStructure.removeRetiredLater` over the box it was placed by, widened to the cell. `onLevelTick` serves retired structures before stale ones, at most one of either a tick, once the region's chunks are loaded and block-ticking. `removeRetired` removes the fluid registration of every cell that holds one (`world.remove`: a reservoir's contents leave with it and its gauges are refunded, as for a broken vessel), clears the interior cells (an interior cell whose walk east or south reaches a retired shell cell of the same origin and kind) and then the shell cells, waits for light, and lets pipes beside them drop their connection. No other block is touched.
- **A machine's design point** (shape fields 8, batch 2026-11-03-rotating-machinery W4). `Duty` (a compound: `Flow`, `Head`, `PolytropicHead`, `Power`, `Poles`, `Drive` and `Steam` by name, `Exhaust`) on a kind with `hasDuty`, read through `ShapeRepair.SavedDuty.resolve`; one that does not read is repaired to its sizes' (`duty (…) -> its size's`), and a machine saved before the field takes its sizes' (`UnitDesign.duty(size, length)`; a pump: the flow at 3 m/s, 100 m, 2 poles, A3). A saved `CONDENSING_TURBINE` becomes the steam turbine at its size's power, `SteamTurbineDesign.DEFAULT_STEAM` and `CONDENSING_EXHAUST` (10 kPa). Its moved nozzles and the BB2 pumps' moved pipe blocks were approved by the owner; the refresh rebuilds them on load.
- **A tower's cell bank** (shape fields 5). `TowerBank` under `TowerBank`, written and read for an induced tower only; an invalid or missing one repairs to one cell. A constructor draft without it (every draft saved before towers) reads one cell.
- **A column's operating point** (shape fields 4). `Operating` reads through `ShapeRepair.Saved.operating` and `SavedOperating.resolve`; a point that does not read or does not fit is repaired to none (`operating point (…) -> none`), and the column still builds and checks, but cannot start.
- **A column's components and turns** (shape fields 2 and 3). `Components` reads by name (`SavedComponents.resolve`, `ColumnComponents.named`); components that do not read are repaired to none (`components (…) -> none`), a column without them builds as before the field. `Turns` reads each role by name through `ROLE_ALIASES`; a bad, repeated or surplus turn is dropped (`turn … -> automatic`), and the nozzle takes its side by itself.
- **A column's member counts** (shape fields 6, batch 2026-11-02-process-simulator S3b; the trim's, shape fields 7, with the condenser `AIR_THEN_WATER`, C1). `CondenserUnits`, `KettleUnits`, `CoolerUnits` and `TrimUnits` sit in `Components`; a design saved before them reads 1 on every step, with no repair named. A count outside 1..`ColumnComponents.MAX_UNITS` reads 1 and is named (`condenser units 7 -> 1`); the kinds and the other counts stay. More cooler counts than pumparounds are cut to the pumparounds (named).
- **A name** (N1). `Name` on every shell cell, as paint is; absent reads unnamed, a value that breaks `DeviceName`'s rule is cleaned and logged (`DeviceNames.read`), never refused.
- **Constructor.** `readDesign(tag, fresh, repairs)` keeps the saved placement and paint where they decode (else the fresh design's), reads the form through `ShapeRepair.form` and the shape through `ShapeRepair.read` from the fields its form builds with; the other forms' fields (a drum's connections and boot, the bank, the trays) and the turns are kept as saved (finding F2), so a form switch and a reload lose nothing. A saved `Column` (the imported design) is read through `ColumnDesignTags`; a skirt below its least is raised (named), and one that does not read is dropped (`column design (…) -> none`). A saved `Name` is read cleaned (`name (…) -> …` when it changed).
- **A process simulator's sent designs.** `ProcessSimulatorBlockEntity` keeps Send to block's designs under `Designs` (`SentDesignTags`) and the column's saved design under `Design` (`ColumnDesignTags`). Each design's shape reads through `readShape` and its column through `ColumnDesignTags`; an entry that does not read (a retired kind above all) is kept as saved and written back, so no change of code drops it (log `equipment_compat process simulator at=… repair=…`). A later Send to block replaces the list whole; the server takes an upload only when it reads whole (`ProcessSimulatorNetwork.receive`). A form this build no longer has proposes the fresh design's shape with the saved placement and paint. A column design keeps its saved tangent length for the other forms (the column's own follows its trays). A failure that escapes is logged and leaves `Design.fresh()`.
- Log lines: `equipment_compat kind=… at=… repair=…` (shell), `equipment_compat at=… repair=paint (…) -> default`, `equipment_compat constructor at=… repair=…`, `equipment_compat status=REMOVED kind=… origin=… shell=… interior=… detail=…`, `equipment_compat removal failed at …`.

<details><summary>Contract: layout refresh (AGENTS.md)</summary>

AGENTS.md: a placed structure whose cells another layout placed is rebuilt to the current layout. Nozzle pipe blocks are kept in place first, then cells; the origin, paint, nozzle sizes and fluid identity are kept; another block is never overwritten. Only the set of cells can be stale; a cell's drawing follows the code at every load.

1. **Stamp.** `ShapeRasterizer.stamp(shape)`: 64-bit FNV-1a over the box size, the shell and interior cells and the ports; never 0. Each shell cell saves it (`Layout`) with its box (`Box`). `stale()` is true when the stamp differs or the cell lies outside the current box. A cell saved before stamps, or written by a blocked refresh, has stamp 0, so it is stale.
2. **Defer.** `onLoad` (server) calls `refreshLater`. It records the region: the box (the larger of old and new) widened by `REACH` + 1 = 4 along x and z and by 1 below and above, so the nozzles' pipe blocks are read, and widened to the cell. Blocks are never changed while a chunk loads.
3. **Tick.** `onLevelTick` (`LevelTickEvent.Post`) serves at most one vessel a tick (a retired one first, then a stale one, then one to restate), the first whose region's chunks are all loaded and block-ticking (`shouldTickBlocksAt`), so block changes reach watching players.
4. **Plan.** `refresh` reads the region: own shell cells by origin and equal shape (`SHELL`), interior cells through `shellReached` (`INTERIOR`, or `OTHER` for another vessel's), fluid device blocks (`PIPE`), other non-replaceable blocks (`OTHER`). `EquipmentRefresh.anchor` picks the x/z shift within `REACH` 3 that keeps the most nozzles on a pipe (the block each port faces out to holds `PIPE`), then covers the most of the vessel's own cells; ties go to the smaller shift, then to lower x and z. The plan clears cells the layout dropped, places missing ones where the world is free, and **never overwrites** a `PIPE` or `OTHER` block (`blocked`, logged).
5. **Rebind.** Every shell cell is rebound to the current shape and keeps its paint (new cells take the first paint read). With blocked cells, every shell cell written calls `layoutIncomplete()` (stamp 0), so the next load refreshes again and places what the player has cleared since. Then `ChunkMap.waitForLightBeforeSending` for each chunk of the region, so a chunk is not sent with stale light.
6. **Registration** (decision D7). `reregister` finds the registration at the old node and submits one queued edit, `Device.withPlacement(new node, new ports)`, for a vessel and a mover alike; a vessel's spec takes the new volume, level height and open flag, a mover's is kept. The device keeps its identity and controls, and the registry keeps a vessel's moles, energy and solids in the new geometry and moves it in the chunk index ([fluid-runtime.md](fluid-runtime.md)). The old node cell releases the identity, the new one binds it. A vessel with no registration registers anew, empty. Pipes beside touched cells update; nozzles redraw. A failure is logged and the cells stay as they are.
7. **Registry first** (batch 2026-11-03-save-corruption, W5 step 6). The registry records each structure by origin (`EquipmentStructure.record`: shape, paint, text, name; written at `place`, `refresh`, `rename` and repaint, removed at `dismantle`). A chunk load queues the structures whose box it touches (`realignLater`); once every chunk of the box and its neighbours is block-ticking, after the layout refresh when that has it queued, `serveRealignment` checks at most 8 a tick. A structure whose cells stand as its record says is left as it is and its holds released (`stands`). Any other is rebuilt to its record where the record puts it (`realign`: `survey`, `EquipmentRefresh.plan` with shift 0, `write` with the record's paint and name, `restoreFluidBinding`); a cell another block holds is left out, never overwritten, and the structure's registrations (`members`) and shaft part (`holdShaft`) are held until a later load finds it whole. Logged as `equipment_realign`. A shell cell that shows a paint or name the record lacks teaches it (`restatePaint`).
- Log lines: `equipment_refresh kind=… origin=… shift=… cleared=… placed=… blocked=…`, `equipment_refresh left N cells out: other blocks stand there, first at …`, `equipment_refresh failed at …`.
- `VesselScreen.sheet` shows "N cells blocked by other blocks …" while a layout cell holds no part of the vessel on the client ([client-gui.md](client-gui.md)).
</details>

<details><summary>Contract: pipe spacing, climbs, moving parts</summary>

- **Pipe spacing** (owner rule 2026-10-17): pipes in blocks that share a face join, so no two pipe blocks of one shape may touch by a face, an edge or a corner. `Nozzle.crowds` tests it for a drum's side connections inside `clashes()`; the gate for every shape is `ProcessUnitTest.pipesApart`, called by `ShapeRasterizerTest`, `ProcessUnitTest` and `ColumnStructureTest`.
- **Climbs**: `Access.MAX_CLIMB` = 9 m between rest platforms (owner decision C1 2026-10-18, columns and units alike; `ColumnStructure.MAX_CLIMB` is the same constant). `Access.climbs` splits a longer climb into equal ones to the nearest metre.
- **Moving parts**: a design marks facets with a part index (`Mesh.part`) and adds a `ProcessUnit.Motion` (pivot, axis, rev/s, phase; a slider-crank when `crank` > 0). The kit's speeds: `FAN_SPEED` 1.5, `PUMP_SPEED` 2, `CRANK_SPEED` 1 rev/s; designs may set slower ones. `MOTION_PERIOD` = 2400 ticks holds whole turns of every speed. Kinds with `moves()` true: air cooler, pump, compressor, centrifugal compressor, steam turbine, motor, generator, gearbox, cyclone, stirred reactor, delayed coker, both fluidized beds, grate boiler, fixed-bed gasifier. Only the client moves them ([client-gui.md](client-gui.md)).

<details><summary>Budgets, caches and determinism</summary>

- **80 000 quads** is the ceiling for one unit at its largest size (60 000 the aim). No test in `src/` pins it; it is measured offline (`tools/new-units/`), and designs coarsen detail to stay under it (`DelayedCokerPlan`, `FluidizedBedBoilerLayout` comments).
- `ShapeRasterizer` geometry cache: 64 shapes or 300 000 facets, then cleared whole. `NozzleBore.largest` (the world's) reads `ShapeRasterizer.inside`, the cache's geometry, as before; `NozzleBore.proposed` (the process simulator's sizing only) searches on `ShapeRasterizer.solid`, the cached geometry where the world holds one, else one built for the search and not kept (a one-slot probe): proposed sizes, shapes no world places, stay out of the cache (`3e39dbc39`, on the sizing's own entry since `58da4714f`). `ProcessUnit` cache: 64. `EquipmentStructure` VoxelShape cache and the client model caches: 4096 keys.
- `serverOnly()` (dedicated server, from the `IChemE` constructor): geometries drop their facets once laid out. Layout, keys, ports, solids and stamps stay; `surface`, `fixedSurface` and `moving` throw `IllegalStateException`.
- All trigonometry in `science/equipment` goes through `DeterministicMath` (`sin`, `asin`, `atan2` among it), so layouts, stamps and solids are bitwise equal on client and server and on every JDK. `DeterministicMathSourceTest` scans `science/`, this package included.
- The pipe fittings, dials and the tank's roof are not chunk-baked from a cell key alone: fittings are baked by `FluidDeviceModel` from the pipe's blockstate and drawn geometry; the roof, the rolling ladder, the tank's level bands, sight-glass liquid and dial needles are drawn per frame by the client renderers ([client-gui.md](client-gui.md)).
</details>

<details><summary>Traps</summary>

- `Kind.unit()` is `ordinal() >= EXCHANGER` and `designed()` is `ordinal() >= TANK`: a new vessel of revolution goes before `TANK`, a new storage vessel between `SPHERE` and `EXCHANGER`, a new unit after it.
- `ConstructorPayload.form` is the `VesselPlacement.Form` ordinal: reordering `Form` needs a protocol bump. NBT stores names. The same holds for `EquipmentShape.Role` in a turn on the wire (`encodeTurns`) and the component kinds on the column wire (`ColumnWire.readKind`, with the member counts after them); NBT stores their names.
- **A column without components is an old column.** `components` null means the column was built before the field (or from a `withTrays`/`withFeed` edit): it keeps its vapour-return nozzle (`reboiled`) and has no assembly (`AssemblyService.designed` is false). A column with components drops the vapour return unless the design has a kettle (Q9 a).
- **The simulator's Save design checks nozzles on the least skirt**, the constructor on its own (`ColumnDesign.misfit` in both): a design saved fine can still be refused at Build on a taller or shorter skirt.
- A turned nozzle tries only its turned side; if that side has no free row it is unplaced, and the constructor will not build the column until the turn is undone (Save design knows no turns).
- **A sent design's count is not built.** The constructor builds one unit of a `SentDesign`; the player builds `count` of them in parallel, and the column's assembly checks that number for a member step ([machine-assemblies.md](machine-assemblies.md)).
- **An import's choice names its index, tag and member.** A Send to block between the list and the choice changes the list; the server then lands nothing (`import_changed`).
- **A name is on every shell cell.** `rename` writes all of them or none; `name(level, …)` reads the controller cell, else the first loaded one. A packet never sets the constructor's name: only an import does (`keepingColumn`), and an untagged design (the saved column design) clears it.
- Renaming or removing a `Kind` without a `ShapeRepair.KIND_ALIASES` entry retires it: every placed structure of it is removed whole on load, registration and contents included. A `Form`, `Heading` or `Orientation` constant needs its alias too (else the constructor proposes the default shape, a shell reads north or vertical).
- A new shape field needs a default equal to the behaviour before it, read in `ShapeRepair.read`, and a new corpus file (`shapes-<n>.snbt`); a changed layout fails `EquipmentLayoutGoldenTest` until `layouts.txt` is updated (the test writes the new table under `build/compat/layouts/`).
- Columns and the units that are neither movers nor heat units are drawing-only and have no fluid binding. A constructed mover's auxiliary nozzles (an oil cooler's, coolant) do not join the network; nor do a heat unit's nozzles that are not a side's inlet or outlet (a fired heater's fuel nozzle; a kettle's feed, vapour and liquid outlets are its shell vessel's ports). Reservoir, mover and heat side registrations are owned by the fluid runtime.
- A heat unit's sides are partners: they register, restate and leave in one event. Half a pair never stands; `restoreFluidBinding` and `restateUnit` register the unit afresh when a side is missing. A side's node is its first inlet's port cell, so a layout change that moves that nozzle moves the side.
- A sphere is a closed reservoir (a drum's physics); only a tank registers open.
- `place` with `rows` < the box height builds part of a vessel and does not register it (debug only).
- `EquipmentStructure.dismantling` is a static server-thread flag: cells removed by a dismantle, a refresh or a retired removal start no removal of their own.
- `find` (and the retired removal's interior walk) walks east, then south, over interior cells for at most `FIND_STEPS` (the widest body, a 40 m tank's, + 2 = 42) steps.
- A pipe block in a cell the new layout needs is left as it stands (`PIPE` blocks the cell like `OTHER`); the vessel then works without that cell and retries at every load.
- The `column` debug command's diameter argument stops at `MAX_DIAMETER` 10, though a column takes 15. The `boot` literal is offered under `vertical` too; the shape then refuses it.
- `MachineAnimation`'s datasheet switch stops every unit's parts, drawn at rest as a stopped mover's; it re-meshes no chunk, holds for the whole client and is not saved.
</details>

## Terms

- **Shell cell / interior cell**: a block that draws and collides with its piece of the vessel / an invisible full cube inside it, with no data (a tank's is `OPEN`: it passes light and collides up to the roof).
- **Storage vessel**: a floating-roof tank or a storage sphere: a reservoir drawn from its parts by a `UnitDesign`.
- **Fitting**: a pipe accessory drawn on its host pipe (valve, sight glass, Y-strainer, inline mover).
- **Cell key**: what a shell cell's model and collision are built from; body cells share one per texture class.
- **Body class**: one of up to eight cell rows (skirt or shell, four course phases) whose cells are identical.
- **Nozzle code**: the 7-bit drawn size of one nozzle in a cell key; 0 = unconnected.
- **Nozzle's largest pipe**: the largest bore a nozzle takes (`NozzleBore`); a pipe's largest, 0.8 m, but on a column, where it is what the neck's place clears.
- **Port**: a nozzle as a fluid port: the flange-face cell, the outward direction, the port class (vapour, bulk, liquid, overflow); a mover's port also has its role (suction or discharge).
- **Stamp**: the hash of a shape's layout that each shell cell saves; another value on load (0 before stamps and after a blocked refresh) means the cells are stale.
- **Repair**: one named change `ShapeRepair` makes to a saved shape the current rules refuse; logged as `equipment_compat … repair=…`.
- **Retired kind**: a saved kind name this build does not have (no alias); its structure is removed whole on load (owner decision D6).
- **Layout table**: `compat/layouts/layouts.txt`, what 81 reference shapes place; `EquipmentLayoutGoldenTest` holds the code to it.
- **Kit**: `ProcessUnit`'s shared pieces; a design may use the kit, never another design's file.
- **Component design**: what a column's plant needs besides the column (`ColumnComponents`), saved with its trays by a process simulator's column (Save design, Send to block) and carried by the built shape.
- **Member count**: how many identical units stand in parallel on a column's heat-transfer step (`ColumnComponents.Units`); a sent design's `count`.
- **Sent design**: a unit's design a process simulator's Send to block stored for the constructor's Import (`SentDesign`).
- **Name / nameplate**: a placed device's player-chosen name, a fact on its block entities (`DeviceName`) / the plate a named structure draws on its shell (`Nameplate`).
- **Home side / turn**: the side a column's side nozzle tries first by itself / the side the player set for it in the constructor.
