## Read this when

- You change how a drum, a distillation column, a floating-roof tank, a storage sphere or a process unit is shaped, drawn, collided with, climbed or placed.
- You add a unit kind, a nozzle, a deck, a stair, a ladder or a moving part.
- You change the drawn shape of a pipe, a pipe fitting (valves, sight glass, Y-strainer, inline pump and compressor), a gauge dial or a constructed mover's flow arrows.
- You touch the equipment constructor, its packets (`equipment-13`) or the shell cells' NBT.
- You change a column's component design (`ColumnComponents`, `ColumnDesign`), where a column's side nozzle stands or which side it faces, or a nozzle's flow direction and arrows (`PortMarkings`). The plant built round a column is in [machine-assemblies.md](machine-assemblies.md).
- A placed vessel looks wrong after a code change (the layout refresh), or a pipe does not meet a nozzle.
- A tool refuses a pipe at a column's nozzle as too large: the largest pipe a nozzle takes (`NozzleBore`).
- A log shows `equipment_compat` (a saved shape repaired, a retired kind removed) or `equipment_refresh` lines; you rename, remove or add a `Kind`, `Form`, `Heading` or `Orientation` constant or a shape field.
- A reservoir's or a constructed mover's fluid registration (ports, volume, level height, open flag) is wrong. The fluid side is in [fluid-runtime.md](fluid-runtime.md).

## In one paragraph

A player places an **equipment constructor** and designs a vessel on its screen: a drum (standing or lying), a column laid out by its trays, a floating-roof tank, a storage sphere, or one of 22 unit kinds (exchanger, air cooler, pump, compressor, heaters, reactors, turbines, FCC converter, delayed coker, boilers, gasifiers). **Build** sends the design. A column is built only from a design a column calculator saved and the constructor imported: its diameter, trays and components; the player keeps the skirt, the placement, the paint and which side a nozzle faces. The server places the vessel whole, as plain blocks: **shell** cells, which carry the shape and draw their own piece of the surface, and invisible **interior** cells. Every surface polygon is clipped to its own cell. A chunk therefore bakes each shell cell like a normal block, from a **cell key**, and no cell reads another cell or chunk. Collision is each cell's solid at 1/8 block. Nozzles take the size of the pipe connected to them, up to a pipe's largest; a column's nozzle takes at most the largest pipe its place clears. A complete drum, tank or sphere registers as one fluid reservoir with its design volume, its level height and a port per nozzle; the tank registers open to the atmosphere. A complete pump, compressor or centrifugal compressor registers one mover at its suction nozzle cell with one suction and one discharge port. A complete exchanger, kettle, air cooler or fired heater registers one **heat side** per passage, each at its first inlet's cell with its inlet and outlet nozzles as ports: an exchanger's tubes and shell, a kettle's tubes, an air cooler's process side, a fired heater's convection and radiant coils; a kettle's shell registers after them as a closed vessel at its pool's node ([heat-exchangers.md](heat-exchangers.md)). Columns and the other units are drawn only; a column and the units round it form an assembly that the server validates against the column's design ([machine-assemblies.md](machine-assemblies.md)). Using a shell cell is decided on the server: a column, or a unit a column's report holds, opens the column's assembly screen; anything else opens its own screen. A tank's floating roof and rolling ladder are drawn by the client and collide through `FloatingRoofs`, at the height the tank's published level gives. Breaking a cell dismantles the whole structure and refunds its gauges. A saved shape is a fact (AGENTS.md, "Saves and data formats"): it is read by name onto the current rules and repaired where they refuse it (`ShapeRepair`); a structure an older layout placed is rebuilt to the current one when it loads, its nozzles kept on their pipes first, and a structure of a kind this build no longer has is removed whole. The geometry code (`science/equipment`) is Minecraft-free and deterministic; it also draws pipes, pipe fittings and gauge dials. The world side is `EquipmentStructure`, `FloatingRoofs` and two block entities. Process contents and operating state belong to the fluid runtime: constructor packets edit static layouts, and process edits and displays follow the engine schedule.

## Main flow

```mermaid
sequenceDiagram
    participant S as EquipmentConstructorScreen
    participant N as EquipmentNetwork (server)
    participant C as EquipmentConstructorBlockEntity
    participant E as EquipmentStructure
    participant F as FluidWorldAuthority
    S->>N: ConstructorPayload(design, build=true)
    N->>C: Design(...) validates, apply(design)
    N->>E: clashes() empty? place(level, origin, shape, paint, all rows)
    E->>E: shell cells bind(shape, cell, paint); interior cells
    E->>F: reservoir: placeVessel(node, ports, volume, levelHeight, open); mover: placeMover(suction, kind, ports) [ledger edit]
    E->>E: reconnect pipes, refreshNozzles
    N-->>S: chat message (built / blocked / clash)
```

1. The screen edits a local draft and sends it as a `ConstructorPayload` with what to do with it: closing sends it with `KEEP`, **Build** with `BUILD`, **Import** with `IMPORT`. Import reads the nearest column calculator's saved design within `IMPORT_REACH` 16 (`importColumn`) onto the draft just applied: the column form, its diameter, trays and components; the draft's placement, paint, skirt and turns stay. Every import is answered with an `ImportAnswer`: the message and the design after it (the `writeDesign` tags), or only why there is none (`no_calculator`, `no_design`, `invalid`, `not_editable`). The screen shows "Importing…" until the answer, then loads the design whole (the message also to chat, naming the trays and feed in the calculator's numbering: trays + 2, feed + 1) or shows the refusal on its status line until the next edit (to chat when the screen has closed).
2. `EquipmentNetwork` checks the player (loaded, within 8 blocks, may build, not a spectator, may interact). It rebuilds the `Design` (`ConstructorPayload.design`), whose compact constructor validates the shape, and keeps the constructor's imported column (`keepingColumn`: a packet never sets it). An invalid design is answered in chat (an import's in its answer).
3. A column without an imported design is refused (`import_first`), and so is one whose side nozzle finds no row on the constructor's own skirt (`ColumnDesign.misfit`, chat `misfit`). A build with side-connection clashes is refused. Otherwise `EquipmentStructure.place` checks every cell first: loaded, inside the build height, replaceable. The first blocked cell is named in an `IllegalStateException`, and nothing is placed.
4. Cells are set with `UPDATE_CLIENTS | UPDATE_KNOWN_SHAPE` (no neighbour shape updates). Each shell cell binds its shape, cell, paint, the layout's stamp and box.
5. A whole vessel with `fluidDevice()` registers through one queued ledger edit. A drum, tank or sphere: `FluidWorldAuthority.placeVessel` at its lowest inside cell, a port per nozzle with the class its `PortClass` gives (a tank's OVERFLOW nozzle becomes an OVERFLOW port), open for a tank. A vessel over `FluidDeviceSpec.MAX_VOLUME` (30 000 m³) is logged and not registered. A pump, compressor or centrifugal compressor: `FluidWorldAuthority.placeMover` at its suction nozzle cell with its SUCTION and DISCHARGE nozzles as ports (auxiliary nozzles, such as an oil cooler's, stay drawn only). A heat unit: `FluidWorldAuthority.placeUnit` with one `UnitSide` per `HeatSides.of` side (HEAT_SIDE at its first inlet's cell, its inlet and outlet nozzles as INLET and OUTLET ports, its passage, a `HeatUnit` with its side and surface) and, for a kettle, a `UnitVessel` at its pool's node with the shell's own nozzles. The node cell takes the identity (`bindIdentity`); a heat unit's every node takes its side's. Then pipes in front of the ports update their connections, and the nozzle cells take the pipes' sizes.
6. Use on any cell is decided on the server (`EquipmentShellBlock.use`). A column, or a unit a column's assembly report holds, opens that column's assembly screen ([machine-assemblies.md](machine-assemblies.md)). Otherwise a reservoir or constructed mover opens its fluid menu (`FluidDeviceMenu`, reach widened by the vessel's size) with a datasheet tab, and another unit gets the datasheet: the server sends `assembly_datasheet` and the client opens `VesselScreen` from the cells it already has. The fluid debugger always opens the device's own screen. Breaking any cell calls `dismantle`.

<details><summary>Frames, boxes and units</summary>

- 1 block = 1 m. A vessel is built in its own frame, axis along +y. Vertical: the front (liquid outlet, skirt opening) is frame −z. Lying drum: frame x is up, y the axis, the front head at y = 0. A unit's frame stands like a vertical vessel's, its front −z towards the constructor.
- `width()` is odd, so the axis runs through cell centres. Every nozzle's centre line does too, and its flange face lies on a block face (`STAND_OUT` 0.15 m at least beyond the surface).
- `transform()` turns the frame by quarter turns to face `front` and shifts the box to start at 0. The origin is the box's lower north-west corner.
- The **controller** is a role, not a block: the lowest shell cell, then westmost, then northmost.
- Vessel grid: drum D 0.5..10 m, column D 0.5..15 m, both in 0.5 m steps; tangent length 1..64 whole m (a column's follows its trays); support up to `MAX_SUPPORT` 16 (a skirt at least `minimumSkirt(D)`, room under the bottom head for the outlet elbow at the largest pipe: 2 m to D 2.5, 3 m to D 6.5, 4 m to D 10.5; or 0 for a bare standing drum; saddles at least 1, centred `SADDLE_POSITION` 1/8 of T/T from each tangent line). A tank, a sphere and the units read diameter, tangent length and support as their own sizes (`UnitDesign`): a tank D 4..40 and shell 4..24 whole m, no support; a sphere D 4..24 whole m on a support of 2..8 m.
- **Drum outlets.** A skirted drum's bottom outlet leaves through its skirt port at the front or at the side the player turns it to (`outlet`, quarter turns; `EquipmentShape.outlets` lists the allowed sides: every side for a skirted drum, the front for a column, a design's own for a tank or a unit); the port, slots and chairs turn with it. A lying drum's outlets all leave at its front end and its feed enters at the far end. A lying drum may take a water boot (the constructor's boot switch) where `bootFor` finds room: every drum from 6 m long and at least as long as wide. The boot is a third of D, just inboard of the front saddle; the oil leaves through a bottom nozzle between the boot and the front head, or low on the front head where there is no room. Where no cell there keeps a pipe's largest flange 0.15 m clear of the front saddle, the nozzle stands on the cell beside the saddle's block column and takes a smaller largest bore (`Boot.largest`, 0.05 m steps; 0.5 m on the eight drum sizes this applies to, 2 x 6 m among them; owner decision 2026-10-28). Without a boot the liquid outlet is low on the front head.
- Shell facets: `max(12, 4⌈k π D⌉)`, k = 0.75 for a drum, 1 for a column. Heads have facets/4 bands.
</details>

<details><summary>Contract: cell keys and nozzle codes</summary>

- `CellKey(shape, x, y, z, nozzles)` is the model's and the collision's key. A **body** cell (wholly in the cylindrical shell or skirt, clear of supports, openings, slots, platforms, manway, boot and every nozzle's reach) replaces its axial coordinate with `BODY − class` (`BODY` = −1, class 0..7: skirt or shell, times four course phases). Callers caching by key build each class once. Cells in a ladder's box column never share a body key.
- `nozzles` holds a 7-bit code for each nozzle reaching the cell, in nozzle order: `PipeSizes.code(od)` (1..64, 1/64 m), 0 = unconnected (drawn at its default size under a blind flange). At most 9 nozzles (`KEYED_NOZZLES`, 63 bits) may reach one cell; a 10th throws when the geometry is built.
- A nozzle's cells are those of its largest size (`PipeSizes.MAX_OUTSIDE` 0.8 m), so the layout never depends on what is connected. Every nozzle neck but a column's, a unit's included, can take a pipe's largest size.
- **A column's nozzle takes the largest pipe its place clears** (`NozzleBore`, owner decision 2026-10-27, option C of `NOZZLE_ROOM_GATE_REVIEW`). F3 sizes a nozzle's room by its design bore, so at a pipe's largest a neck can meet a ladder, a rail or the cladding. The search runs from the design pipe up in 0.05 m steps and stops at the first neck that meets the rest of the column; a neck whose drawn outside reaches `MAX_OUTSIDE` gives `MAX_BORE`. The laying tool's anchor refuses a larger line (`PipeAnchor.Refusal.NOZZLE_SIZE`) and its port label shows the nozzle's own largest ([pipe-laying.md](pipe-laying.md)); the sizing tool refuses a pipe or a line beside a nozzle that takes less (`nozzle_too_small`, `PhysicalRegistry.NOZZLE_TOO_SMALL`; [instrument-accessories.md](instrument-accessories.md)). Pinned on the gated columns: the 0.5 m column's side nozzles 0.25 m (the neck wider than the cladding), the 1.5 m column's reflux 0.7 m, the 3 m column's reflux 0.55 m (a ladder beside it); the others a pipe's largest.
- The server writes the codes (`refreshNozzles`: the connected pipe's `PipeSizes.outside(bore)`, 0 without a pipe) and syncs a cell whose key changed. `refreshNozzlesAt` runs from `EquipmentShellBlock.neighborChanged` (next server task) and from `FluidDeviceBlockEntity.refreshGeometry`.
- A pipe connects to a shell cell only where `nozzleFacing(face)` is non-null and the pipe has a joint on that face (`FluidDeviceBlock.connects`), for every kind. A hand-placed pipe has every face; a laid one only the faces its route uses ([pipe-laying.md](pipe-laying.md)). Only a reservoir's ports and a constructed mover's suction and discharge exist in the fluid topology.
- Every kind's IN and OUT nozzle necks carry one band of four flow arrows each (`PortMarkings.surface`, pointing with the stream that `PortMarkings.flow` gives the role), baked with the cell; the band stops where the neck is hidden (`ShapeRasterizer.hides`), and a code of 0 draws it at the design size. A storage tank's or sphere's ports carry none. They are drawing only; the assemblies read the same `flow` ([machine-assemblies.md](machine-assemblies.md)).
</details>

<details><summary>Contract: collision at 1/8 block, ladders, light</summary>

- `ShapeRasterizer.solid(key)` samples the cell at the centres of 8³ voxels (`SOLID_RESOLUTION` 8) and greedy-merges them (z, then x, then y). `EquipmentStructure.collision` joins the boxes into one `VoxelShape`; both sides compute the same shape. An unbound shell cell is a full block.
- A wall a player must meet is at least one voxel thick: `Access.RAIL_BAND` 0.2, `RUNG_FACE` 0.06, `NARROW_BAND` 0.13 between two fences, `FENCE` 1.4 m high. `Access.PLAYER` and `MIN_DECK` (1.45 m) follow from the voxel size.
- `isLadder` asks `ShapeRasterizer.climbable` at the entity's position (in front of the rungs, inside the cage). Every cell a climb zone reaches is a shell cell, drawn or not (`climbCells`).
- A ladder lands through an opening in its deck's handrail, `Access.WALK_THROUGH` (0.425 m) either side of its line: a player and one voxel each side. A wound stair (`Access.WoundStair`, on tanks and spheres) has a fence either side and is open on its inner side where it meets a deck.
- Shell cells are transparent to light (`getLightBlock` 0, skylight passes). Interior cells are opaque and cast the shadow, except a tank's (`EquipmentInteriorBlock.OPEN`): they pass light so the roof shows from above, collide only up to the roof's walking surface (`FloatingRoofs`), and are a ladder along the rolling ladder's line.
- **A skirt that shows its inside** (`ShapeRasterizer.skylight`: a drum's or a column's hollow skirt, a unit's skirt with openings, `ProcessUnit.openSkirt`; owner request 2026-10-27, batch 2026-10-27-skirt-shadow): the vessel's interior cells are `EquipmentInteriorBlock.SKYLIGHT`. They stay solid and hide the faces beside them, but pass light, so the floor inside the skirt takes the skylight of the open ground beside the vessel. Opaque, they left that floor lit only from the skirt ring, one level less a block inwards (level 9, 54 % at noon, at a 10 m drum's centre). A save from before reads `skylight=false`; the controller cell's load queues `EquipmentStructure.restateLater`, and `restate` (one vessel a tick, after retired and stale ones, once its chunks are loaded and block-ticking; only a current, own vessel) sets each interior cell holding another state, then waits for light (`waitForLightBeforeSending`). A refresh restates the interior cells it keeps. No cell is added or removed and the stamp does not change. Log line `equipment_restate kind=… origin=… interior=…`.
- The roof and the rolling ladder stand where the tank's published level puts them (`TankDisplay.Glide`, the same height on server and client at the same game tick); before the first display the roof stands at the node cell's saved `RoofLevel`. The first display after a load glides from that level over one bucket (`FloatingRoofs.standing`, `TankDisplay.Glide.next`), so a plant that filled or drained while its chunk was unloaded carries its riders up or lets them follow down, as `LIFT` (0.6 m) allows; it does not jump. A rising roof lifts what stands on it (`FloatingRoofs.carry`, from `LevelTickEvent.Post` on the server and the client tick for the player).
</details>

<details><summary>Contract: saved shapes and retired kinds (ShapeRepair)</summary>

AGENTS.md, "Saves and data formats": an equipment shape, its paint and nozzle sizes are facts. Shape fields are append-only, and enums are saved by name.

- **Read by name.** `EquipmentShellBlockEntity.saved(tag)` takes the fields as written (`ShapeRepair.Saved`: names, numbers, null for an absent array); `ShapeRepair.read` gives a `Read`: a valid shape or a retired kind name, and each repair as `field from -> to`. It never throws. A `Kind`, `Heading`, `Orientation` or `Form` name goes through its alias map (all empty at this snapshot). A missing field reads its default, the behaviour before the field existed: a drum saved without `Connections` gets `EquipmentShape.defaultConnections` (the standard inlet), with no repair named.
- **Repairs.** An unknown front reads north, an unknown drum orientation vertical, undecodable trays 20 fed at 10, undecodable connections none, an undecodable bank one bay. Then, while the shape is refused, the ladder runs in order: sizes (a unit snapped to its own grid and range, a drum or column to 0.5 m and its range, the length a whole number in range; non-finite values take the kind's default), support into its range (a boot drum's at least `minimumBootSupport`), connections off the shell or past `MAX_CONNECTIONS` dropped, the outlet to the front, the boot removed, the bank to one bay, and last the kind's default size with the saved front. A repaired shape has another stamp, so the normal refresh rebuilds the structure.
- **Retired kind** (owner decision D6). A kind name this build does not have: the cell stays unbound, keeps its saved tag (`retiredData`, written back unchanged), and its `onLoad` (server) queues `EquipmentStructure.removeRetiredLater` over the box it was placed by, widened to the cell. `onLevelTick` serves retired structures before stale ones, at most one of either a tick, once the region's chunks are loaded and block-ticking. `removeRetired` removes the fluid registration of every cell that holds one (`world.remove`: a reservoir's contents leave with it and its gauges are refunded, as for a broken vessel), clears the interior cells (an interior cell whose walk east or south reaches a retired shell cell of the same origin and kind) and then the shell cells, waits for light, and lets pipes beside them drop their connection. No other block is touched.
- **A column's components and turns** (shape fields 2 and 3). `Components` reads by name (`SavedComponents.resolve`, `ColumnComponents.named`); components that do not read are repaired to none (`components (…) -> none`), a column without them builds as before the field. `Turns` reads each role by name through `ROLE_ALIASES`; a bad, repeated or surplus turn is dropped (`turn … -> automatic`), and the nozzle takes its side by itself.
- **Constructor.** `readDesign(tag, fresh, repairs)` keeps the saved placement and paint where they decode (else the fresh design's), reads the form through `ShapeRepair.form` and the shape through `ShapeRepair.read` from the fields its form builds with; the other forms' fields (a drum's connections and boot, the bank, the trays) and the turns are kept as saved (finding F2), so a form switch and a reload lose nothing. A saved `Column` (the imported design) is read through `ColumnDesignTags`; a skirt below its least is raised (named), and one that does not read is dropped (`column design (…) -> none`). A form this build no longer has proposes the fresh design's shape with the saved placement and paint. A column design keeps its saved tangent length for the other forms (the column's own follows its trays). A failure that escapes is logged and leaves `Design.fresh()`.
- Log lines: `equipment_compat kind=… at=… repair=…` (shell), `equipment_compat at=… repair=paint (…) -> default`, `equipment_compat constructor at=… repair=…`, `equipment_compat status=REMOVED kind=… origin=… shell=… interior=… detail=…`, `equipment_compat removal failed at …`.
</details>

<details><summary>Contract: layout refresh (AGENTS.md)</summary>

AGENTS.md: a placed structure whose cells another layout placed is rebuilt to the current layout. Nozzle pipe blocks are kept in place first, then cells; the origin, paint, nozzle sizes and fluid identity are kept; another block is never overwritten. Only the set of cells can be stale; a cell's drawing follows the code at every load.

1. **Stamp.** `ShapeRasterizer.stamp(shape)`: 64-bit FNV-1a over the box size, the shell and interior cells and the ports; never 0. Each shell cell saves it (`Layout`) with its box (`Box`). `stale()` is true when the stamp differs or the cell lies outside the current box. A cell saved before stamps, or written by a blocked refresh, has stamp 0, so it is stale.
2. **Defer.** `onLoad` (server) calls `refreshLater`. It records the region: the box (the larger of old and new) widened by `REACH` + 1 = 4 along x and z and by 1 below and above, so the nozzles' pipe blocks are read, and widened to the cell. Blocks are never changed while a chunk loads.
3. **Tick.** `onLevelTick` (`LevelTickEvent.Post`) serves at most one vessel a tick (a retired one first, then a stale one, then one to restate), the first whose region's chunks are all loaded and block-ticking (`shouldTickBlocksAt`), so block changes reach watching players.
4. **Plan.** `refresh` reads the region: own shell cells by origin and equal shape (`SHELL`), interior cells through `shellReached` (`INTERIOR`, or `OTHER` for another vessel's), fluid device blocks (`PIPE`), other non-replaceable blocks (`OTHER`). `EquipmentRefresh.anchor` picks the x/z shift within `REACH` 3 that keeps the most nozzles on a pipe (the block each port faces out to holds `PIPE`), then covers the most of the vessel's own cells; ties go to the smaller shift, then to lower x and z. The plan clears cells the layout dropped, places missing ones where the world is free, and **never overwrites** a `PIPE` or `OTHER` block (`blocked`, logged).
5. **Rebind.** Every shell cell is rebound to the current shape and keeps its paint (new cells take the first paint read). With blocked cells, every shell cell written calls `layoutIncomplete()` (stamp 0), so the next load refreshes again and places what the player has cleared since. Then `ChunkMap.waitForLightBeforeSending` for each chunk of the region, so a chunk is not sent with stale light.
6. **Registration** (decision D7). `reregister` finds the registration at the old node and submits one queued edit, `Device.withPlacement(new node, new ports)`, for a vessel and a mover alike; a vessel's spec takes the new volume, level height and open flag, a mover's is kept. The device keeps its identity and controls, and the registry keeps a vessel's moles, energy and solids in the new geometry and moves it in the chunk index ([fluid-runtime.md](fluid-runtime.md)). The old node cell releases the identity, the new one binds it. A vessel with no registration registers anew, empty. Pipes beside touched cells update; nozzles redraw. A failure is logged and the cells stay as they are.
- Log lines: `equipment_refresh kind=… origin=… shift=… cleared=… placed=… blocked=…`, `equipment_refresh left N cells out: other blocks stand there, first at …`, `equipment_refresh failed at …`.
- `VesselScreen.sheet` shows "N cells blocked by other blocks …" while a layout cell holds no part of the vessel on the client ([client-gui.md](client-gui.md)).
</details>

<details><summary>Contract: pipe spacing, climbs, moving parts</summary>

- **Pipe spacing** (owner rule 2026-10-17): pipes in blocks that share a face join, so no two pipe blocks of one shape may touch by a face, an edge or a corner. `Nozzle.crowds` tests it for a drum's side connections inside `clashes()`; the gate for every shape is `ProcessUnitTest.pipesApart`, called by `ShapeRasterizerTest`, `ProcessUnitTest` and `ColumnStructureTest`.
- **Climbs**: `Access.MAX_CLIMB` = 9 m between rest platforms (owner decision C1 2026-10-18, columns and units alike; `ColumnStructure.MAX_CLIMB` is the same constant). `Access.climbs` splits a longer climb into equal ones to the nearest metre.
- **Moving parts**: a design marks facets with a part index (`Mesh.part`) and adds a `ProcessUnit.Motion` (pivot, axis, rev/s, phase; a slider-crank when `crank` > 0). The kit's speeds: `FAN_SPEED` 1.5, `PUMP_SPEED` 2, `CRANK_SPEED` 1 rev/s; designs may set slower ones. `MOTION_PERIOD` = 2400 ticks holds whole turns of every speed. Kinds with `moves()` true: air cooler, pump, compressor, centrifugal compressor, both turbines, cyclone, stirred reactor, delayed coker, both fluidized beds, grate boiler, fixed-bed gasifier. Only the client moves them ([client-gui.md](client-gui.md)).
</details>

<details><summary>Formats and versions</summary>

- **Shell block entity NBT**: `Kind`, `Diameter`, `TangentLength`, `Orientation`, `Front`, `Support`; `Trays` (column, with `Components`: `Condenser`, `RefluxPump`, `PumparoundPumps` by name, `Ejector`, `Kettle`, written when present, shape fields 2; and `Turns`: a list of `Role` by name, `Index`, `Side`, written when not empty, shape fields 3) or `Connections` + `Boot` (drum); `Outlet` (quarter turns); `Bank` (air cooler); `Cell`, `Nozzles` (long), `Paint` (int[2]), `Layout` (stamp), `Box` (int[3]); `FluidIdentity` on a reservoir's or mover's node cell only (not in the update tag); `RoofLevel` on a tank's node cell (the roof's last level, written when above 0); the gauges' `AccessoryRevision` and `Instruments`. `Kind`, `Orientation` and `Front` are lower-case names. Shape fields are append-only facts: they are read through `ShapeRepair` (missing fields at their defaults, refused values repaired and logged, an unknown kind retired); an invalid `Paint` reads `Paint.DEFAULT`, logged. A cell or box past 512 is refused: the cell logs a warning and stays unbound. A retired cell saves its tag back unchanged.
- **Constructor NBT**: `Diameter`, `TangentLength`, `Form`, `Support`, `Offsets` (forward, right, up), `Trays`, `Connections`, `Paint`, `Bank`, `Boot`, `Outlet`; `Column` (the imported design, `ColumnDesignTags`) and `Turns` when present. Read by `readDesign` field by field (placement and paint kept where valid, the shape through `ShapeRepair`, an unknown form the fresh shape); a design with no `Diameter`, or one the repair cannot read, gives `Design.fresh()` (2 m × 4 m standing drum on its default skirt, 20 trays fed at 10, the config paint).
- **Corpus and table** (AGENTS.md): `src/test/resources/compat/equipment/shapes-1.snbt` holds the saved shell tags of shape fields revision 1 (27 shapes: every kind at its default size and a lying drum); `shapes-2.snbt` (28: adds a column with components) and `shapes-3.snbt` (29: adds a turned column with components and turned nozzles) follow; `EquipmentShapeCompatTest.SHAPE_FIELDS` = 3. A shape-field change raises `SHAPE_FIELDS` and adds `shapes-<n>.snbt`. `src/test/resources/compat/layouts/layouts.txt` records 81 layouts (re-recorded at F3, `ef0ae447`: the reflux pipe block of the three column rows moved, and the vapour return's of two) (drums standing and lying and columns at three sizes, each designed kind at its smallest, middle and largest size): box, cell counts, stamp and each nozzle's pipe block. A layout change updates it; a moved nozzle pipe block needs the owner's approval.
- **Wire** `equipment-13`: `ConstructorPayload(position, diameter, tangentLength, form ordinal, support, forward, right, up, trays ≤ 5+4+2+8, connections ≤ 18, paint 2, bank 2, boot, outlet, turns ≤ 3·MAX_TURNS (`ColumnTrays.encodeTurns`), action)` with `Action` by ordinal (`KEEP`, `BUILD`, `IMPORT`); the payload carries no column design. `IMPORT` reads the nearest `ColumnCalculatorV3BlockEntity` within `IMPORT_REACH` 16 (its saved design; nothing is solved). `ImportAnswer(position, message, optional design tags)`, server to client.
- **Fluid registration**: a reservoir is a RESERVOIR device with `PhysicalFluidTopology.Port`s (cell, face, phase port, role VESSEL; at most `MAXIMUM_PORTS` 64) and a `FluidDeviceSpec` carrying the design volume (≤ `MAX_VOLUME` 30 000 m³), `levelHeight` (≤ `MAX_LEVEL_HEIGHT` 128 m) and `open` (a tank). A constructed mover is a PUMP or COMPRESSOR device whose node is its suction cell, with exactly one SUCTION and one DISCHARGE port (BULK). A heat side is a HEAT_SIDE device at its first inlet's cell with INLET and OUTLET ports (BULK), its passage geometry and its `HeatUnit` (unit, side, surface, partner, control, heat in transit); a kettle's shell is a closed RESERVOIR carrying a `HeatUnit` of side SHELL. All are facts of the fluid checkpoint's topology unit (envelope 1, state format 29, facts schema 5; layout in [fluid-runtime.md](fluid-runtime.md)), so they survive a state-format change.
- **Config** `[equipment]`: `primaryColour`, `secondaryColour` (#RRGGBB) and `metallicSheen` (0..0.4, default 0.2; read when models bake).
</details>

<details><summary>Budgets, caches and determinism</summary>

- **80 000 quads** is the ceiling for one unit at its largest size (60 000 the aim). No test in `src/` pins it; it is measured offline (`tools/new-units/`), and designs coarsen detail to stay under it (`DelayedCokerPlan`, `FluidizedBedBoilerLayout` comments).
- `ShapeRasterizer` geometry cache: 64 shapes or 300 000 facets, then cleared whole. `ProcessUnit` cache: 64. `EquipmentStructure` VoxelShape cache and the client model caches: 4096 keys.
- `serverOnly()` (dedicated server, from the `IChemE` constructor): geometries drop their facets once laid out. Layout, keys, ports, solids and stamps stay; `surface`, `fixedSurface` and `moving` throw `IllegalStateException`.
- All trigonometry in `science/equipment` goes through `DeterministicMath` (`sin`, `asin`, `atan2` among it), so layouts, stamps and solids are bitwise equal on client and server and on every JDK. `DeterministicMathSourceTest` scans `science/`, this package included.
- The pipe fittings, dials and the tank's roof are not chunk-baked from a cell key alone: fittings are baked by `FluidDeviceModel` from the pipe's blockstate and drawn geometry; the roof, the rolling ladder, the tank's level bands, sight-glass liquid and dial needles are drawn per frame by the client renderers ([client-gui.md](client-gui.md)).
</details>

<details><summary>Traps</summary>

- `Kind.unit()` is `ordinal() >= EXCHANGER` and `designed()` is `ordinal() >= TANK`: a new vessel of revolution goes before `TANK`, a new storage vessel between `SPHERE` and `EXCHANGER`, a new unit after it.
- `ConstructorPayload.form` is the `VesselPlacement.Form` ordinal: reordering `Form` needs a protocol bump. NBT stores names. The same holds for `EquipmentShape.Role` in a turn on the wire (`encodeTurns`) and the component kinds on the column wire (`ColumnV3Network.readKind`); NBT stores their names.
- **A column without components is an old column.** `components` null means the column was built before the field (or from a `withTrays`/`withFeed` edit): it keeps its vapour-return nozzle (`reboiled`) and has no assembly (`AssemblyService.designed` is false). A column with components drops the vapour return unless the design has a kettle (Q9 a).
- **The calculator checks nozzles on the least skirt**, the constructor on its own (`ColumnDesign.misfit` in both): a design saved fine can still be refused at Build on a taller or shorter skirt.
- A turned nozzle tries only its turned side; if that side has no free row it is unplaced, and the constructor will not build the column until the turn is undone (Save design on the calculator knows no turns).
- Renaming or removing a `Kind` without a `ShapeRepair.KIND_ALIASES` entry retires it: every placed structure of it is removed whole on load, registration and contents included. A `Form`, `Heading` or `Orientation` constant needs its alias too (else the constructor proposes the default shape, a shell reads north or vertical).
- A new shape field needs a default equal to the behaviour before it, read in `ShapeRepair.read`, and a new corpus file (`shapes-<n>.snbt`); a changed layout fails `EquipmentLayoutGoldenTest` until `layouts.txt` is updated (the test writes the new table under `build/compat/layouts/`).
- Columns and the units that are neither movers nor heat units are drawing-only and have no fluid binding. A constructed mover's auxiliary nozzles (an oil cooler's, coolant) do not join the network; nor do a heat unit's nozzles that are not a side's inlet or outlet (a fired heater's fuel nozzle; a kettle's feed, vapour and liquid outlets are its shell vessel's ports). Reservoir, mover and heat side registrations are owned by the fluid runtime.
- A heat unit's sides are partners: they register, restate and leave in one event. Half a pair never stands; `restoreFluidBinding` and `restateUnit` register the unit afresh when a side is missing. A side's node is its first inlet's port cell, so a layout change that moves that nozzle moves the side.
- A sphere is a closed reservoir (a drum's physics); only a tank registers open.
- `place` with `rows` < the box height builds part of a vessel and does not register it (debug only).
- `EquipmentStructure.dismantling` is a static server-thread flag: cells removed by a dismantle, a refresh or a retired removal start no removal of their own.
- `find` (and the retired removal's interior walk) walks east, then south, over interior cells for at most `FIND_STEPS` (the widest body, a 40 m tank's, + 2 = 42) steps.
- A pipe block in a cell the new layout needs is left as it stands (`PIPE` blocks the cell like `OTHER`); the vessel then works without that cell and retries at every load.
- The `column` debug command's diameter argument stops at `MAX_DIAMETER` 10, though a column takes 15. The `boot` literal is offered under `vertical` too; the shape then refuses it.
- `MachineAnimation`'s datasheet switch stops every unit's parts, drawn at rest as a stopped mover's; it re-meshes no chunk, holds for the whole client and is not saved.
</details>

## Terms

- **Shell cell / interior cell**: a block that draws and collides with its piece of the vessel / an invisible full cube inside it, with no data (a tank's is `OPEN`: it passes light and collides up to the roof).
- **Storage vessel**: a floating-roof tank or a storage sphere: a reservoir drawn from its parts by a `UnitDesign`.
- **Fitting**: a pipe accessory drawn on its host pipe (valve, sight glass, Y-strainer, inline mover).
- **Cell key**: what a shell cell's model and collision are built from; body cells share one per texture class.
- **Body class**: one of up to eight cell rows (skirt or shell, four course phases) whose cells are identical.
- **Nozzle code**: the 7-bit drawn size of one nozzle in a cell key; 0 = unconnected.
- **Nozzle's largest pipe**: the largest bore a nozzle takes (`NozzleBore`); a pipe's largest, 0.8 m, but on a column, where it is what the neck's place clears.
- **Port**: a nozzle as a fluid port: the flange-face cell, the outward direction, the port class (vapour, bulk, liquid, overflow); a mover's port also has its role (suction or discharge).
- **Stamp**: the hash of a shape's layout that each shell cell saves; another value on load (0 before stamps and after a blocked refresh) means the cells are stale.
- **Repair**: one named change `ShapeRepair` makes to a saved shape the current rules refuse; logged as `equipment_compat … repair=…`.
- **Retired kind**: a saved kind name this build does not have (no alias); its structure is removed whole on load (owner decision D6).
- **Layout table**: `compat/layouts/layouts.txt`, what 81 reference shapes place; `EquipmentLayoutGoldenTest` holds the code to it.
- **Kit**: `ProcessUnit`'s shared pieces; a design may use the kit, never another design's file.
- **Component design**: what a column's plant needs besides the column (`ColumnComponents`), saved with its trays by a column calculator and carried by the built shape.
- **Home side / turn**: the side a column's side nozzle tries first by itself / the side the player set for it in the constructor.


## Optional tank level-gauge accessory (feature 2026-10-26)

TankDesign.gauge now writes into an accessory Mesh, not the tank fixed mesh. ShapeRasterizer.tankGauge caches the transformed surfaces; TankGaugeGeometry.bounds derives the same full-height service extent without drawing. Existing custom glass/rails/caps/pipework/scale retained. The physical tank keeps its ports/volume/datum and native refresh; no new collision block or simulation node. Tank fitting position is fixed beside the front ports and recomputes from shape on refresh.
