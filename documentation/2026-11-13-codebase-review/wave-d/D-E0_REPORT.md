One test answers a fixed list of requests, one line each, about 430 lines (the test prints the exact count and a tally of statuses, classifications, balance, band, Newton, outer and deposition counts). The comparison is line by line and fails on the first line that differs, with the request's number, its id and both lines; a count mismatch names the first extra or missing id.
2. **A line** is `<engine> <request> | <answer>`. The answer holds the specification, status, unsupported reason, then the classification (with `merged` / `near-pure` markers) or the typed failure kind (`CRITICAL_BAND[+UNSTABLE_PRODUCT|UNRESOLVED_PRODUCT]`, `UNSTABLE_PRODUCT`, `UNRESOLVED_PRODUCT`, `CRYSTAL_PHASES`, `CRYSTAL_STEP`, `REFUSED`, `OUTSIDE_BOX`, `UNTYPED`) and the grade. Then come every count of `Diagnostics` (feed verdict, flash, extrapolation, Rachford-Rice, kernel, derivative, Newton and outer counts, the band and balance flags) and the raw bits of its six doubles. Then, per phase: kind, crystal, T, P, the bits of each nonzero amount, the root and its count, v, h, s, g, water's three shares, and ln phi for the request's species (fluid phases only). Then the coexisting states of a pure coexistence, the H, U, V, S aggregates, and the deposition record. Onsets print status, reason, crystal, T, P, approximation, residual, fluid count, grade and the fluid answer. A refusal by exception prints `throws <class>`. A round trip whose TP had no answer prints `skipped`. A NaN prints as `NaN` (its payload is platform-dependent); every other double prints as hex raw bits.
3. **Counts are included** because the existing bitwise-repeat tests already treat them as stable: `FluidTpEquilibriumTest` determinism, the P3 reused-workspace test and `AqueousEquilibriumTest.kernelEvaluationsRecorded` compare flash, extrapolation, Rachford-Rice, kernel, derivative, Newton and outer counts with `coexistenceFromBalance`. The engine reads no clock and no thread count (no `nanoTime`, `currentTimeMillis`, `Thread` or parallel stream under `science/thermo`), and every budget is an iteration count. Each engine answers its requests on one workspace, in list order.
