## 2. The physics, then what changed

**Expected.** One design on one feed is one column: it solves or it does not, whatever screen shows it, and its condenser removes what the column's energy balance leaves, on the basis its streams are booked on. A sheet books every stream with the network's flashes (`FlowThermo`, row 17), as a running column books its accounts, so its condenser duty must be the closure on those flashes, or the sheet's heat balance carries the two property packages' basis gap as a gap.

**What the code did.** The sheet solved by its own route (rows 8 to 10): a second, learned cold solve when the shared call failed, and a learned first solve where the world takes V3's fast classical one. So a design could solve on the sheet and be refused in the world, or the reverse. It booked V3's own condenser duty against streams on the network's basis and read the difference out as `energy_gap`, which was the sheet's heat gap (FlowsheetColumnTest 154 pinned the two equal to 1 W).

**What is one now** (commits `aa8b39bf4`, `58d4f73a3`):

- `ColumnSolve.calculate(input, seed, catalog, screen, checkpoint)` (ColumnSolve 181): the world's call, moved out of `solve` with its seed screen (`ColumnSolve.warm` 204, the old 258-266 as a function), bodies unchanged: `calculateWithStrategy(input, AGGRESSIVE, warm, screen)` inside `MaterialRuntime.with(catalog, input.packageId())`. The world calls it at 298, the block at `ColumnBlockSolve` 361, the sizing's trials at `ColumnSizing` 165. Both sheet callers pass `ColumnUnit.SCREEN` (55), V3's default ratio. `ColumnUnit.solve`, its learned fallback and `START` are deleted.
- `ColumnSolve.closure(out, in, reboiler, pumparounds)` (194): `out - in - reboiler - pumparounds`. The world's condenser (350) is `closure(out, in, solved.reboilerPerFeed() * rate, paTotal) / rate`: the same operations in the same order as the old line 319. The block's `whole` (ColumnBlockSolve 161) is `closure(heatOut, heatIn, Qr, Σ stage heat)` on its own streams: the feed as flashed to its tray, the steam supplies, the ejector's motive steam in and its free water out (so the motive heat is inside the closure, as it was in V3's duty less `motiveHeat`). The condenser readings, the water share (`trim`), the free side's duty and the wired side's link all take it.
- `energy_gap` is gone: with the closure it is zero by construction. V3's own duty stays on the block's display (`V3ColumnDisplayResult`), as the world keeps `solvedCondenser`.

**The sheet's balance closes.** The block's own residual `heatIn + whole + Qr + Qs - heatOut` is 0 to rounding. On the sheet, `heatGap_new = heatGap_old - energy_gap_old` (the booked condenser duty moves by exactly the old gap), and the old tests held `|heatGap_old - energy_gap_old| < 1 W`. Mass and components are untouched (the same V3 streams are written). Neither stop rule fired. The world's arithmetic is unchanged: the nested `MaterialRuntime.with` sets an equal context, and `input.componentBasis()` is the `basis` object the old screen compared with.

## 3. What stayed two, and why

- **S1, feed water (row 6), can still change solvability.** The world lets water that V3 does not admit as steam follow the distillate through its split table. The sheet writes V3's own streams and has no table to carry water V3 does not model, so it refuses an aqueous liquid and sends all its water to V3 as steam, with no superheat test. Unifying needs the sheet to route that water itself (for example onto the distillate at its state): a new sheet behaviour, an option for the owner.
- Input building (rows 3 to 5): the sheet's door is absolute and its streams are its own; the world scales set ratios by what it took. Each side's own, as the brief says.
- The screen value (row 12): one rule, two values only where a server operator moves `columnV3LiquidSupplyScreenRatio`; the simulator runs on the client and `science` reads no config.
- Failures and published results (rows 14, 20): each side's report.
