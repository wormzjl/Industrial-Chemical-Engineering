# Effects visual review (tmp/fx-record/run1, SHADER path, snowy plains, 2026-11-10)

**Verdict on "looks weird, like a plume":** agreed. The flare flame is a smooth, soft-edged, brushed ribbon whose silhouette never changes (only its texture scrolls), its last third streams almost horizontally, and it runs straight into a flat dark smoke band; it reads as a lit smoke plume, not a turbulent jet flame.

Pixel change between frames (fraction of pixels moving by >12/255): flare flame region f0→f20 20 % (texture scroll inside an unchanged outline); heater-stack plume balls 4 %; induced-tower haze 0.2 %; still door 1.8 %; natural tower 0.7 %.

## Defects, most severe first

1. **Flame reads as a plume** — flares, flare-tip (every frame; full.png). High. Soft airbrush edge, long streaks running the length of the flame, a fixed outline; a real 30 m sooty flare is a ragged, lobed, bright yellow-orange body whose tip tears off and whose length pulses 1–2 times a second. Causes, verified in code: `fx_flame.fsh:59` noise domain is `across*1.6` by `along*3` over a 30 m by 5 m flame, so features are ~6× longer than wide (the streaks); `fx_flame.fsh:64-65` edge `smoothstep(0.55, 1.0)` is a 0.45-wide fade (the blur); `fx_flame.vsh:46-58` only breathes the width ±15 % and sways ≤0.25 half-width (no envelope motion); `FlameModel.java:70` control point at 0.7 of the rise makes the end tangent `(0.8 Δx, 0.3 Δy)`, near-horizontal at u∞/Uj 0.05–0.1. Fix: pass the flame length (e.g. in the unused pilot slot of `params`, or as the ribbon's `UV2` scale) and sample noise in metres with ~2 m features, isotropic; edge `smoothstep(0.85, 1.0)` with a second, coarser noise that cuts lobes off the top third; in the vertex shader add a travelling wave `sin(along*6 − FxTime*8)` of amplitude 0.6 half-width and a length pulse `1 + 0.12 sin(FxTime*7 + seed)` applied to the distance from the tip; move the control point to `(0.3 Δx, 0.85 Δy)` so the tip keeps ~40° of rise.

2. **Plumes start tens of metres downwind and end as 40–65 m balls; natural towers show nothing** — heater-stacks (the two grey balls top centre, all frames), induced-towers (sky-filling haze band), flares (pale mass right third), natural-tower (no plume on either tower, all frames). High. A cold-day tower plume is a dense white column from the fan rim or the throat, billowing, bending downwind, fading over ~100–200 m. Verified in code: `PlumeModel.java:101-104` `distance()` returns `5 D / f` for any f < 1, so an exhaust just under saturation (bench 0.036 kg/kg vs 0.0384 saturated at 36 °C, start f ≈ 0.93) begins 5.4 D downwind: ~45 m for a fan stack, ~130 m for the natural throat; `PlumeModel.java:34` CAP 200 m with `FxSources.java:63,251` 32 puffs / (3 phases × exits) gives 2–10 segments, each up to 100 m, whose puff half-size `D/2 + 0.15 k` (`:184`) reaches 20–33 m (`FxPackedSink` clamps at 32.767 m) and grows 50 % more in the shader. Fix: `distance = max(0, 5 D (1/f − 1))` (zero at the exit, 5 D at f = 0.5); cap the visible length at ~80 m (or fade alpha ∝ 1/k past 40 m); size puffs `D/2 + 0.1 k` capped at 3 D; set the segment count from puff spacing (length / puff diameter), not from the budget, and let the budget thin phases instead.

3. **Vanilla clouds paint over flames and smoke** — flares, flare-tip, heater-stacks, induced-towers (white cloud blocks across the flames and the black smoke, every frame). High. Clouds at y 192 are far behind the effects and should be hidden by them. Verified in code: `FxBatch.java:126` draws at `AFTER_PARTICLES`; vanilla `LevelRenderer` (neoforge-21.1.219 sources, lines 1210-1219) renders clouds after that stage, with a depth-only pass first; `FxRenderTypes.java:64-68` effects test depth but write none, so cloud fragments pass over them. Fix: draw the batch at `AFTER_WEATHER` (clouds have written depth by then, so effects behind clouds still hide correctly); under Fabulous bind the main target before drawing, since that stage runs inside the weather target.

4. **Far plumes drawn over near flames** — flares full.png (the four right flames greyed by the tower plume behind them at z 74), heater-stacks (dark soot blob over the flare flame). Medium. Verified in code: `FxBatch.java:68` `SHADER_GROUPS` draws all flames, then all smoke and plumes, in registration order, with no depth write and no sort. Fix: sort `visible` back to front by camera distance each frame and draw in that order, switching shader when the kind changes (or one shader with a kind flag so flames and puffs interleave in one pass).

5. **Smoke is a string of beads; still flues show no smoke** — heater-stacks (row of faint discs below the balls, y ≈ 700–780 of full.png), stills and drum-wiring (a band of discs at the horizon, y ≈ 120–140 / 210–260), stills (nothing above any brick flue, all frames). Medium. Real stack smoke is a continuous tattered ribbon that widens with distance. Verified in code: `PlumeModel.java:184` sizes every puff of a segment by the segment's start distance, so a one-segment trail never widens (still flue: length 12 m, `segments()` → 1 at 5 m/s wind, three 0.2 m puffs); `FxSink.java:60,71-74` caps the drift at 16 m per period, so puffs of a 20–100 m segment travel 16 m and vanish, leaving gaps; `FxSources.java:388` oil smoke density 0.18 → puff alpha 0.1. Fix: put the per-puff growth in the vertex (old half = size at the segment start, new half = size at its end, the shader mixing by phase) instead of the fixed +50 %; store the drift in millimetres like the sizes and drop the 16 m cap; set segments from `length / (2 × half-size)` with at least 3; draw smoke as a noise-eroded ribbon (the flame technique with the plume shader) and keep puffs only for the far end.

6. **Visible creases across each flame at segment joints** — flares full.png (faint cross-lines on every flame at regular intervals), flare-tip. Medium. Verified in code: `FxPackedSink.java:61-75` gives all four vertices of a segment that segment's own axis, and `fx_flame.vsh:52-56` builds the side vector from it, so two segments meeting on a curve have different side vectors and their widths disagree at the joint. Fix: write each end's tangent (mean of the two adjacent segment axes) as that vertex's normal; the sink has both points, `FlameModel.ribbon` can pass the previous and next centre.

7. **Flare soot smoke is a flat dark fog band, not trails** — flares, overview (a horizontal black layer above the flame ends, same shape every frame). Medium. A sooty flare trails a dark, tattered streamer that thins to brown-grey. Verified in code: `FxSources.java:314` starts the smoke at 2 × flame half-width (5.4 m puffs growing to 9 m) with `SMOKE_RATIO .3` (`:65`), so neighbouring trails 7 m apart merge; `fx_plume.fsh:54,61-64` draws each puff as a sun-lit sphere with a soft rim. Partly a scene artefact (10 flares in a row). Fix: start at 1 × half-width with per-puff growth (item 5), erode puffs harder (`0.45` → `0.9`, higher-frequency noise), flatten the sphere lighting (`+ 0.3` → `+ 1.0`).

8. **Still door fire is a static icon, the opening stays black** — stills (every frame; 1.8 % pixel change in the door over 2 s). Low. A full solid fire lights the whole door opening orange and flickers visibly. Verified in code: `StillModel.java:20,67-69` ember band 0.25 of the door height at heat 0.35; flames `w * .12` wide; `fx_flame.vsh:58` sway is 0.25 half-width × along², sub-pixel on a 0.3 m flame. Fix: an ember/glow ribbon over 0.6 of the opening at heat 0.6 and opacity 0.7; sway amplitude in metres (0.3 × flame height) for ribbons shorter than 1 m.

9. **Pilots never show** — flare-tip (nothing at the rim, all frames). Low. Real pilots are three small blue-yellow flames visible at the rim when the main flame leans away. Verified in code: `FlameModel.java:77-78` root half-width is `max(0.26 × 0.09 L, tip)` = 0.7 m at L 30 m, wider than the 0.6 m pilots, which `write()` draws under the inner ribbons (`:88-89`). Fix: root half-width = tip radius (`profile` → 0 at v = 0), pilots drawn last, 1 m long.

Drum wiring: no defect visible, but at this distance the conduit relief cannot be judged; a closer shot (≤ 3 m, low sun) is needed. Heat haze was off (`heat_haze` default false), so nothing here judges it.

## Check by the orchestrating session (2026-11-10)

Reviewer: Fable, high effort, at the owner's request. Each cited line re-read; the frames re-viewed independently.
- Items 1, 3, 4, 5, 6, 9: confirmed in code and frames. Item 3 against vanilla: `LevelRenderer` dispatches `AFTER_PARTICLES` (line 1210), draws clouds (1219), then rain and `AFTER_WEATHER` (1225–1226). Moving the batch puts rain behind the effects; acceptable.
- Item 2: the code is as stated. But `5 D / f` is the right centre-line law (pure exhaust in the potential core, about 5 D). What the frames lack is the shear layer: a real plume forms at the rim at once. So `5 D (1/f − 1)` is a visual model, not forced by physics, and goes to the owner as an option. Same for the 80 m cap: real cold-day tower plumes run longer, so the cap trades realism for overdraw.
- Item 2, second cause the review missed: in the natural-tower shot, both towers are 124 and 133 blocks from the camera. The client watches towers only within `WATCH_RANGE − 8` = 120 blocks (`ProcessStates.SIGHT`), so it had no state and drew no plume. A 40 m natural-draft tower's plume is the most visible thing in a plant; owner option: a longer watch range for natural-draft towers only.
- Item 7: agreed, partly a scene artefact.
- Item 8: agreed.
- Drum wiring: not confirmed either way. No cable shows on any drum face in the frames, and the shot did not frame a data-port side. The next recording needs a shot within 3 m of one drum's data port.
- Cost link: the bench's first lanes measured effects at +1.32 ms p50 in view (budget 0.3), with the GPU frame doubled. The 20–33 m puffs of item 2 fill the screen with overdraw, so item 2's fixes should also cut GPU time.
